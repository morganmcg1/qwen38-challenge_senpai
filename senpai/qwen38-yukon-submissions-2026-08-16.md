## Executive summary

The public campaign moved from a measured serial-control record of
`0.9992101011` to a correctness-green frontier of `2.9042110287`: a `2.9065x`
score increase (`+190.65%`). Across the promoted metrics, mean candidate time
fell from `0.038025178` to `0.016777262` seconds per emitted token, a `55.88%`
reduction or `2.2665x` throughput increase. The final score is `0.095789`
below the track's operator-set `3.0` plausibility gate. That threshold is an
administrative, fail-closed publication check—not a physical or theoretical
limit on attainable speedup. A median above it is rejected as a measurement
fault or benchmark escape rather than clamped or accepted.

That progress did not come from one trick. The durable frontier is a stack of
four kinds of work:

1. **Do not throw away correct work.** K=1 rejection retained the already
   verified primary; wider rejection moved from large eager checkpoints to a
   compact recurrence-input tape and exact replay; proposal history eventually
   flushed K/V only instead of re-running the whole head.
2. **Spend approximation only on proposals.** A compact proposal vocabulary
   removed `60.4%` of proposal readout rows, and the declared 4-bit/group-64
   proposal head cut the artifact from `849,400,347` to `238,934,093` bytes
   (`71.9%`). The fixed target still produced every emitted token and every
   exact top-two value.
3. **Reduce repeated bytes and mandatory reductions.** Promoted kernels pack
   projections, share affine-4 weights across verify rows, fuse Q/K
   normalization/layout/RoPE and GDN preparation, replace a generic vocabulary
   selection chain with exact reducers, and reuse required top-one evidence.
4. **Schedule the real graph, at the real shape.** The adaptive policy prices
   each extra draft from conditional acceptance; warmups execute the live
   selector and long-prefix variants; asynchronous evaluation starts device
   work while Swift is still constructing later layers.

The equally important result is epistemic: small leaderboard deltas are not
automatically causal. Six groups of byte-identical full Git trees were scored
more than once. Their within-tree score spreads ranged from `0.175%` to
`2.110%`; the largest absolute spread was `0.050354`. A `+0.0026` promotion can
contain useful code, but one receipt cannot establish a sub-percent effect.
Composite submissions, stale-base grafts, and infrastructure retries require
source-lineage analysis before attributing a gain.

## Quantitative picture

### Frontier trajectory

The score history has three distinct regimes:

- **Serial to viable K=1 (`0.9992` → `1.3764`).** Retain the verified primary
  after rejection, pack target projections, remove seed-wide discarded logits,
  and compute exact top-two evidence once.
- **Persistent history and multi-draft (`1.3764` → `2.5246`).** Keep proposal
  context, keep draft IDs on device, price adaptive depth, share affine-4
  weights across rows, use a compact proposal vocabulary, and make recurrent
  rollback proportional to the accepted prefix.
- **Cheap proposal head and late target kernels (`2.5246` → `2.9042`).** Swap
  in the immutable 4-bit proposal head, bridge the width-5 SDPA boundary,
  widen cross-row sharing, flush proposal history as K/V only, fuse
  attention/GDN preparation, reduce selector synchronization, and refine
  scheduling/host-device overlap.

The cumulative final editable snapshot differs from the first receipt's parent
in only 11 paths (`+3,670/-131` lines): the session, head attachment, target
protocol, Qwen target/MTP models, attention/cache helpers, an affine-4 Metal
header and generated twin, and the proposal-head declaration/README. The large
number of attempts converged to a comparatively small executable surface.

## What the whole corpus teaches

### 1. Proposal quality and proposal cost must be optimized together

The proposal side is the safe place to trade exactness for economics because
the target verifies every token. Three results make the point:

- the first compact-vocabulary attempt (`2bb525d`) had the right 98,330-row
  subset but did not warm the exact live argmax/map expression and scored
  `1.9169` against a `1.9547` parent;
- the corrected live-expression warmup (`7b33621`) promoted to `2.2008`; and
- changing only the proposal-head manifest to the 4-bit/group-64 artifact
  (`9174327`) moved `2.5246` to `2.7151`, the largest clean late-stage jump.

The head is not free: lower precision can make drafting cheaper but may reduce
acceptance. The repeated 2-bit readout/head experiments did not produce a
promotion and their scored variants regressed materially. “Fewer proposal
bytes” is useful only while enough good drafts survive.

### 2. Reuse state at the narrowest valid boundary

Winning history is a progression in ownership:

- reuse the target's already-correct primary row after a K=1 rejection;
- retain the proposal head's committed context instead of rebuilding it;
- retain recurrence inputs and replay only the committed prefix after a wider
  rejection instead of materializing every large state boundary; and
- for the one-layer proposal head, flush old committed history through K/V
  preparation only and fully evaluate the current row.

Later code also replaced a whole-model width split with a cache-once,
SDPA-only split. This is the general lesson: preserve the full semantic
boundary, then remove work only inside the component that actually needs a
different shape.

### 3. Memory traffic wins when arithmetic order is protected

The clean target-side promotions do not weaken target math. They share packed
affine-4 weights across adjacent rows, concatenate compatible output
projections, and fuse preprocessing while retaining per-element accumulation
order, BF16 cast points, lower-token-ID tie order, NaN handling, and exact
fallbacks for other shapes. The most reliable target-kernel gain,
`84583d4`, left every per-prompt draft length unchanged while improving the
score by `0.1344`, strongly locating the benefit in verify compute rather than
policy behavior.

Launch-count claims alone were weak. Successful fusions removed a weight walk,
an intermediate, a mandatory reduction, or a dependency edge. Several
compiled wrappers and broader fusions failed or scored below their parents.

### 4. Warmups are part of the algorithm

Warm shape is not enough. The lineage successively learned to warm:

- the 512-row charged seed path;
- legal verify widths behind a real 512-token cache prefix;
- the compact proposal projection plus its exact selector and ID map; and
- replay/rollback and long-KV variants that do not appear behind an eight-token
  toy prefix.

The failed-first/successful-second compact-vocabulary pair is direct evidence:
warming nearby compact logits did not warm the live selection graph. A warmup
must execute the same expression, layout, and context family used after the
timer starts.

### 5. Policy tuning is prompt-distribution tuning

Static depth and one-constant retunes were brittle. After the 4-bit head
changed proposal economics, isolated lower head-cost ratios encouraged excess
depth and produced large regressions on several scored receipts. The promoted
policy instead keeps conditional acceptance estimates per draft position,
limits unobserved optimism, opens deep widths after evidence, and finally caps
the first-position estimate with the already available target top-two margin.

The weakest prompt makes the reason visible: `449` non-drafting rounds protect
it from paying for a head that is unlikely to help. The strongest prompts
still draft above five on average. One global K cannot serve both regimes.

## Current frontier and the next defensible questions

At cutoff, the final editable snapshot already contains persistent head
history, K/V-only flush, exact rollback/replay, multi-row affine-4 sharing,
compact proposal selection, adaptive 0…8 depth, width-wall handling, exact
top-two reduction, fused GDN/full-attention preparation, and decode/seed
asynchronous ladders. Re-proposing any of those as a fresh idea would be a
lineage error.

The corpus supports four next questions without pretending they are already
wins:

1. **Can the hard prompt fall back even closer to the fast serial path?** The
   final policy already skips most rounds there; any further policy must improve
   that prompt without cutting the central two ratios that determine the
   median.
2. **Which part of the final composite is causal?** Margin-aware
   scheduling, wide/seed async ladders, long-input Q/K fallback, and compiled
   SwiGLU landed together. Isolating them is necessary before extending them.
3. **Can attention reuse K/V without crossing the register/occupancy cliff?**
   Grouped-SDPA receipts had local evidence but no completed official result at
   cutoff. The implementation must retain exact per-head order and pass an M5
   resource gate before any performance claim.
4. **Can GDN prework cover the still-common narrow widths?** The promoted
   packed mixer covers widths 3…9. Extending it to 1–2, or folding its remaining
   beta production, is a precise boundary question; pending receipts are not
   evidence of success.

The operator-set `3.0` publication gate is `3.30%` above the current `2.9042`
score multiplicatively, but that is not an estimate of the model's physical
headroom. A legitimate result above the gate would still fail under current
policy and would require operator review. Future work should remain small and
attributable: a large unisolated stack can trip the gate, hide a regression
behind a different prompt mix, or make a failure impossible to diagnose.

## Reproduction notes and limitations

The report can be reproduced from the public sources without model weights:

```bash
yukon submissions --all
yukon submission-note <submission-id>
git fetch upstream 'refs/heads/submissions/*:refs/remotes/upstream/submissions/*'
git show --stat upstream/submissions/<full-uuid>
git diff upstream/submissions/<full-uuid>^ \
  upstream/submissions/<full-uuid> -- <changed-paths>
```

The audit does not infer hidden runner errors, thermal causes, or per-component
speedups when Yukon did not publish them. It does not treat a local M3/M4
microbenchmark as M5 authority, and it does not turn a submission note's claim
into fact. It records dynamic statuses only at the timestamp at the top.

Finally, official correctness establishes exact emitted tokens, rows, and
evidence for the scored tree. It does not prove that every comment in that tree
is correct, that every bundled component helped, or that the same delta will
repeat on a second timing draw. Those distinctions are the main reason this
audit inspected source rather than summarizing the leaderboard prose.
