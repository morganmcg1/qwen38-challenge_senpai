==============================================================================
IS 295 GB/s REAL?  Yes, and it is not even stressed on that box.
==============================================================================
  The ranked box is m5-max-128gb-3 (.github/workflows/qwen-mtp-ranked-benchmark.yml:196 and :1370).
  That is an M5 MAX with 128 GB, not a base M5. Its predecessor part
  already reaches several hundred GB/s, so neither 295 GB/s nor
  590 GB/s is self-evidently impossible there. The F5 section 3
  implausibility argument therefore does not carry, but FINDING 375
  does not need it. Use the disproportion test instead:

  local  second stream = 28.605 ms of a 128.001 ms round = 22.35 % of it
  equal-share prediction for the ranked round        10.93 ms
  measured ranked effect                             -0.20 ms
  disproportion                                     -0.018 x

  No uniform bandwidth scaling can turn 22 % of a round into 0 %.
  A faster bus shrinks the stream and the rest of the round together.
  Only a change in WHERE the second stream is served can remove it,
  so FINDING 375's cache-absorption reading survives on stronger
  evidence than the 590 GB/s figure.

  The same effect is already visible on my own host, at half strength:
  a second 14.418 GB stream costs 28.605 ms, an effective 504.0 GB/s,
  which is 1.97 x my MEASURED 255.5 GB/s DRAM peak. Roughly half
  of the second pass is already cache-served on an M4 Pro. The M5 Max
  finishes the job.
