CHECK 4 - IS IT BANDWIDTH BOUND?
==============================================================================
    per packed byte (2 weights): 4*M FLOP of matvec + 4*G FLOP of
    dequantisation, against 1 byte of weight and 1/16 byte of
    bf16 scale and bias. AI ~ 4*(M+G)/1.0625.
    The advisor's 'AI ~ 5' is the SERIAL number: at M=1, G=1 it is
    7.5. Arithmetic intensity rises with verified rows, which is the
    whole point of batching the verify pass.
    shipped@d4     W=5 G=1  AI ~  22.6 FLOP/byte   knee ~ 28 FLOP/byte
    minna5@d4      W=5 G=2  AI ~  26.4 FLOP/byte   knee ~ 28 FLOP/byte
    shipped@d5     W=6 G=2  AI ~  30.1 FLOP/byte   knee ~ 28 FLOP/byte
    machine balance uses 7.2 TFLOP/s fp32 (20 cores x 128 lanes x 2 x 1.4 GHz), an ESTIMATE, over the
    measured 255 GB/s.

==============================================================================
B, THE MARGINAL VERIFIED ROW, AGAINST THE WEIGHT STREAM
==============================================================================
  One extra verified row reads NO extra weights. The batch grows by
  one activation row and one output row per projection, and nothing
  else. Across the 257 routed dispatches of a verify pass:
    extra bytes per row        12,640,256  = 12.64 MB
    at the measured 255 GB/s that is 0.049 ms
    extra FLOP per row               51.2 GFLOP (2 x 25.62e9 weights)
    B measured                      7.002 ms
    B is 142x its own byte cost, so B is not a bandwidth term at all.
    B implies 7.32 TFLOP/s against an estimated 7.2 TFLOP/s fp32 peak
    = 102% of peak. Above 100 %
    means the peak estimate is a little low, not that the row
    broke physics: the clock and the lane count are both guesses.
    Either way the marginal row sits on the FLOP roofline and not
    on the bandwidth roofline. Lowering h needs arithmetic, not
    traffic.
    local B / ranked h = 7.002 / 5.3351 = 1.3125
    This is a clean local-to-ranked row-channel transfer factor:
    both sides are one extra verified row and nothing else.

==============================================================================
WHAT IS THE HEADROOM WORTH, AND WHAT COULD EXPLAIN IT?
==============================================================================
  shipped@d4 W=5: 92.39 ms measured, 74.17 ms at the size-matched rate.
  Headroom 18.23 ms/round = 19.7% of the round, not 43%.
  Thorfinn measures a 1.03 ms host-side GPU-idle window per
  round. That is 6% of this headroom, so the idle
  window and the missing bandwidth are NOT the same phenomenon at
  this width. Most of the gap is inside GPU execution.

  The round has two regimes, and they answer the question
  differently:
    whole round   205 / 255 GB/s = 80% of bandwidth, 3.14 / 7.2 TFLOP/s = 44% of compute
    marginal row  1.8 GB/s = 0.7% of bandwidth, 7.32 TFLOP/s = 102% of compute
  The base of the round streams weights and is bandwidth limited.
  The rows stacked on that base are compute limited. That is why
  neither 'wasting 43 % of bandwidth' nor 'purely compute bound'
  describes it, and it is why a kernel change that saves traffic
  helps the base while a kernel change that saves arithmetic helps
  the per-row term.
