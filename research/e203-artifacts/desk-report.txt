1  QUESTION
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Does a prompt's accepted-length stream carry serial structure that a causal
within-prompt rule could turn into extra ranked score, and if it does, is the
ceiling of that structure worth an implementation?

The comparator is NOT receipt A.  FINDING 523 established that the best static
global cap is 8, worth 3.829386, which is +3.28% over receipt A (paid, cap 7,
3.70784519415395).  A within-prompt rule must beat cap 8, because cap 8 costs
nothing to implement.  The assignment's promotion bar is +0.5% over cap 8.

of within-prompt serial dependence on local prose and prices the ceiling with
ranked cost laws.  A hidden prompt could in principle be more autocorrelated
than any of these 9.  Section 6 shows how far it would have to be.

3  STAGE 0a  is there any structure?      artifacts stage0a-p7.{json,txt}
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
All tests are against a within-prompt permutation null (exchangeability), so a
prompt's own marginal acceptance rate cannot create a false positive.

  autocorrelation of accepted length, arm p7, 1741 rounds pooled
    lag 1                      rho1 = +0.0014   (null sd 0.0239)
    max over lags 1..16               +0.0429 at lag 15, p = 0.286
                                      against the max-over-lags null
  drift, first half vs second half of each prompt, vs block-16 null
    pooled                            +0.0514, p = 0.058
    sign agreement                    7/9 positive, binomial p = 0.180
                                      technical +0.262, benchfixture -0.220
  one-step-ahead prediction skill vs the causal running mean
    EMA, alpha in 0.05..0.70          NEGATIVE at every alpha
                                      best alpha 0.05 -> -0.0018
    causal expanding-window AR(L)     NEGATIVE at every order
                                      best L = 1 -> -0.0173
  the shipped tracker's own forecast
    corr(EMA forecast, realized accepted length), 1741 rounds
                                      +0.0085   (95% band +-0.047)
                                      only benchfixture (+0.269) tracks
  leave-one-prompt-out
    rho1 range                        -0.019 .. +0.017
    EMA skill range                   -0.008 .. +0.001

Reading.  The p7 accepted-length stream is compatible with exchangeability at
every lag tested.  Neither an EMA nor a causal AR beats the running mean, so
there is nothing for a lookahead rule to condition on.  The drift term is the
only marginally interesting statistic and it fails its own sign test.

STRUCTURE BUDGET.  Converting the 95% upper confidence bounds into the share
of accepted-length variance a perfect causal predictor could explain:

    from rho1                         psi <= 0.2324%
    from best-of-16-lags              psi <= 0.8033%

Stage 0a therefore fires the first branch of the stopping rule on its own.
Stage 0b was still run, because a small psi is only worthless if the score is
also insensitive to it, and that has to be priced rather than assumed.

4  THE CLOSED-LOOP ARM IS A TRAP     artifacts stage0a-adapt.{json,txt},
                                               censoring-control.{json,txt}
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
The adapt arm looks like it has strong structure:

    rho1 = +0.1167 (p < 1e-4),  EMA skill +0.010 (positive),
    AR(1) skill +0.033,  tracker corr +0.2512

A positive control settles what that is.  research/e203_censoring_control.py
feeds the replayed shipped closed loop (E128 costModelDepth +
recordAcceptOutcome) a provably exchangeable input: the same prompt's
fixed-depth-7 run lengths, permuted, 400 draws.  Anything the output shows is
manufactured by the rule itself.  Two accountings for a censored round are
reported; `carry` is the physically correct one, because a run remainder that
exceeds the offered depth is accepted on the following round rather than lost.

    statistic          shipped   sim discard   sim carry
    lag-1 acf           0.1168      0.0457      0.0980
    tracker corr        0.1662      0.1361      0.1725

The loop manufactures 84% of the observed lag-1 autocorrelation and all of the
observed tracker correlation out of an input with none.  Simulator fidelity is
good (e.g. english: mean depth 2.337 observed / 2.259 simulated, accepted
1.081 / 1.129, censored fraction 0.317 / 0.337), so the control is credible.

The one prompt where observed far exceeds manufactured is benchfixture, and
benchfixture is 79% truncated even at fixed depth 7, so its p7 input is itself
censored and the control does not apply there.

Conclusion: the adaptive arm's apparent within-prompt structure is an artefact
of depth feedback plus censoring.  Any experiment that measures acceptance
autocorrelation on a closed-loop trace will find structure that is not there.

    [step9: const global d=4 3.570432; const per prompt 3.607890;
     cap8 3.681378; oracle_q 3.781543 +2.72%; oracle_a 4.120362 +11.92%]

oracle_a is the assignment's literal oracle: it is told the realized run length
before it picks the depth.  It is not attainable and it is not the ceiling for
a causal rule; it is quoted so the gap to oracle_q is visible.

oracle_q is the right upper envelope for a rule that predicts the round's
latent acceptance rate perfectly.  It is worth +3.51% over cap 8.  That is the
value of PERFECT within-round information, i.e. psi = 100%.

THE E203 ANSWER.  A causal rule cannot have psi = 100%; Stage 0a bounds psi at
0.23%..0.80%.  Interpolating the ceiling on the shipped cap-8 base at the
measured psi:

    psi                       ceiling      vs cap 8
    0.2324%  (rho1 bound)    3.829726      +0.009%
    0.8033%  (any-lag bound) 3.830560      +0.031%
    1%                       3.830848      +0.038%
    5%                       3.836666      +0.190%
    10%                      3.843878      +0.378%
    25%                      3.865119      +0.933%
    50%                      3.899306      +1.826%
    100%     (= oracle_q)    3.963760      +3.509%

BREAK-EVEN.  The evidence a within-prompt rule would need to clear the bars:

    to reach +0.2% over cap 8   psi >= 5.26%    [step9: 7.40%]
    to reach +0.5% over cap 8   psi >= 13.29%   [step9: 18.49%]

Measured 95% upper bound on psi is 0.2324% (lag 1) to 0.8033% (best of 16
lags).  The shortfall is a factor of 6.5x to 22.6x for the +0.2% bar and 16.5x
to 57x for the +0.5% bar.  This is not a near miss.

------------------------------------------------------------------------------
6  HOW WRONG WOULD THE TRANSFER CAVEAT HAVE TO BE?
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
For the hidden prompts to reach even the weakest useful bar, their accepted
length would need a causal predictable variance share of 5.26% against the
0.23% measured here at lag 1.  In rho1 terms that is rho1 ~ 0.23 sustained,
against +0.0014 measured, i.e. the same magnitude as the value the closed-loop
control MANUFACTURES from an exchangeable input.  Nothing in the 9-prompt
spread supports that: the per-prompt rho1 range on p7 spans a band consistent
with zero, and leave-one-prompt-out moves the pooled estimate by less than
0.02.  The corpus is local prose and the hidden set is also prose; the two are
not so different that a 100x change in serial dependence is credible.

8  SECONDARY FINDING, reported because it is uncomfortable
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
On the open-loop p7 corpus the SHIPPED EMA tracker's own forecast correlates
+0.0085 with the realized accepted length over 1741 rounds, inside a +-0.047
band.  The tracker is not tracking anything within a prompt.  The E201/E197
instrument nevertheless credits the shipped adaptive rule with +2.27% over the
best constant depth (3.829386 vs 3.744526).  Both statements can be true: the
instrument's credit can come from the rule's BETWEEN-prompt and marginal-rate
behaviour, or from the depth trajectory it happens to walk, rather than from
any within-prompt prediction.  E203 does not resolve which, and it is out of
this assignment's scope to resolve it.

SUGGESTED FOLLOW-UP, NOT IMPLEMENTED
  Test whether replacing the within-prompt EMA adaptation with a well-chosen
  constant or purely between-prompt depth policy is neutral or better on the
  ranked score.  This sits on the E199 cap-8 axis, it is a SUBTRACTION rather
  than an addition, and it needs a GPU confirmation because the instrument
  cannot arbitrate its own credit assignment.  If the tracker really collects
  nothing within a prompt, removing it is free simplification; if it collects
  something, the mechanism is not the one its name implies and it is worth
  knowing what it is.
